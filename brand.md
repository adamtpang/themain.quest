## Palette: Hero Meadow

**Vibe:** grassy overworld, fresh starts, grounded optimism

**Category:** consumer/social

**Mood:** playful and warm

Hero Meadow is an original whimsical-fantasy system. It uses bright natural color, dark botanical ink, irregular shapes, and tactile game feedback. It must not copy recognizable characters, logos, locations, props, or scenery from existing entertainment properties.

## Typography: Space Grotesk + Space Mono

- **Display and body:** Space Grotesk
- **Mono, numbers, scores, and system labels:** Space Mono
- **Special pixel moments:** Press Start 2P and VT323 remain available, but never use them for paragraphs or dense UI

The production fonts are self-hosted through `next/font/google` in `app/layout.tsx`. CSS variables `--font-sans` and `--font-mono` are available across Tailwind.

## Visual language

### Shapes and borders

- Use 3 px to 4 px botanical-ink outlines for hero cards and important buttons.
- Use slightly irregular corner combinations to feel drawn rather than mechanically rounded.
- Use pill shapes for statuses, filters, and tiny rewards.
- Let cards overlap a path or landscape layer when hierarchy benefits from it.
- Keep dense data cards simpler than decorative quest cards.

### Illustration and iconography

- Build an original world from hills, clouds, paths, stars, campfires, maps, stones, plants, portals, and tiny friendly creatures.
- Prefer simple filled shapes with strong outlines and one memorable silhouette.
- Use Lucide icons inside the product UI, with sticker-like containers for important actions.
- Never reproduce recognizable entertainment characters, costumes, weapons, logos, locations, or signature props.

### Motion

- Interaction feedback should complete in 120 ms to 240 ms.
- Buttons may compress by 2 px to 3 px as their shadow collapses.
- Rewards may pop, bob once, or release a small burst.
- Avoid infinite motion except quiet ambient elements such as a slow cloud or a tiny breathing marker.
- Honor `prefers-reduced-motion` and remove decorative movement when requested.

### Layout hierarchy

1. One boss quest owns the strongest outline, contrast, and action.
2. Today's score and visible proof sit beside or immediately below it.
3. The century-to-today map supplies context without competing for attention.
4. Sidequests and history move below the fold.
5. Meta-work always receives lower visual weight than real-world outcomes.

## Tone and voice

### Words to use

Use friendly, direct language that makes action feel playable: quest, path, proof, party, map, trail, campfire, boss, checkpoint, afterglow, unlock, ship, choose, begin, and close. Speak to the user as an intelligent friend. Keep jokes specific and light.

### Words to avoid

Avoid leverage, utilize, synergy, optimize your potential, hustle, crush it, game-changing, and manufactured urgency. Avoid childish baby-talk, generic fantasy prose, unexplained memes, and language borrowed from recognizable fictional worlds.

### Voice example

> Pick one quest. Make the proof visible. Then collect the afterglow.

## Usage rules

**Do:**

- Use semantic utilities such as `bg-background`, `text-foreground`, and `bg-primary`.
- Keep to the 4 px spacing grid.
- Use one clear focal action per viewport.
- Use `font-mono tabular-nums` for scores, XP, countdowns, and changing values.
- Test every component in light mode, dark mode, and a 390 px viewport.
- Keep illustrations original and subordinate to the user's next action.

**Do not:**

- Hardcode component colors when an existing semantic token fits.
- Use `transition: all`.
- Mix more than one gradient on the same screen.
- Put paragraph text over the accent gradient.
- Apply pixel fonts to body copy.
- Make every card loud, outlined, animated, or decorative.
- Copy recognizable assets or naming from an existing entertainment property.
